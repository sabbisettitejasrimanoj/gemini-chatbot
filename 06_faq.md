# Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: How do I know if I should size up or down?**
A: For athletic shoes, size up slightly if you're between sizes to allow room for foot swelling during activity. For dress shoes, size down slightly for a snugger fit, since leather stretches over time.

**Q: Can I put running shoes in the washing machine?**
A: It is not recommended. Machine washing can damage midsole cushioning and weaken the adhesive bonds between the upper and outsole. Hand washing with a soft brush is a safer alternative.

**Q: How often should I replace my running shoes?**
A: Most running shoes lose significant cushioning after 300 to 500 miles (approximately 480 to 800 km) of use. Visible signs of wear include a compressed or uneven midsole and worn tread patterns.

**Q: What does "drop" mean in a running shoe?**
A: Drop refers to the height difference between the heel and forefoot of the midsole, measured in millimeters. A lower drop (0–4mm) encourages a more midfoot or forefoot strike, while a higher drop (8–12mm) is more common in traditional cushioned trainers.

**Q: Are wide-width shoes just longer versions of standard shoes?**
A: No. Wide-width shoes are built on a different last (the mold used to shape the shoe) that adds width across the ball and toe box while keeping the same length, rather than simply scaling up the entire shoe.

**Q: Why do new leather shoes feel stiff?**
A: New leather has not yet conformed to the shape of your foot. Conditioning the leather and wearing the shoes for short periods initially helps the material soften and mold to your foot shape over time.

**Q: Is it normal for suede to change color when it gets wet?**
A: Yes, suede often darkens temporarily when exposed to moisture. Once fully dry, brushing the nap with a suede brush typically restores the original color and texture, though repeated exposure can cause permanent staining.

**Q: Can waterproof shoes still let my feet sweat?**
A: Yes. Waterproofing prevents external water from entering but does not eliminate perspiration. Breathable waterproof membranes, like Gore-Tex, are designed to let some water vapor escape while blocking liquid water from entering.

**Q: What's the difference between cushioning foam types?**
A: EVA foam is lightweight and affordable but compresses faster over time. PU foam is heavier but more durable. Premium TPU-based foams offer higher energy return and are typically found in performance-oriented shoes at a higher price point.

**Q: Do resoling services work for all shoes?**
A: Resoling is generally only possible for shoes built with methods like Goodyear welt construction, where the sole is stitched rather than glued. Cement-constructed shoes, common in athletic and casual footwear, are usually not designed to be resoled.
