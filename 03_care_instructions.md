# Footwear Care Instructions

## Leather Shoes

Wipe leather shoes with a soft, dry cloth after each wear to remove dust and surface dirt. For deeper cleaning, use a damp cloth with a small amount of saddle soap, working in circular motions, then let the shoes air dry away from direct heat. Once dry, apply a leather conditioner or cream to prevent cracking, and buff with a horsehair brush to restore shine. Avoid soaking leather shoes in water, and never dry them near a radiator or heater, as direct heat causes the leather to crack and warp.

## Suede Shoes

Use a suede brush to gently lift dirt and restore the nap after each wear, brushing in one direction. For stains, a suede eraser can lift marks without damaging the texture. Apply a suede protector spray before first wear and periodically thereafter to guard against water and stains. Never use water or saddle soap on suede, as it will darken and stiffen the material permanently.

## Canvas Sneakers

Remove laces and insoles before cleaning. Use a soft brush with a mixture of mild detergent and warm water to scrub the canvas upper, then rinse with a damp cloth. Air dry at room temperature, stuffing the shoes with paper towels to help them keep their shape and absorb moisture. Avoid machine washing or drying, as the heat and agitation can weaken adhesives and cause the sole to separate from the upper.

## Athletic Mesh Shoes

Hand wash mesh uppers with a soft brush and mild soap; avoid submerging foam midsoles for extended periods, as prolonged water exposure can degrade cushioning foam over time. Remove insoles to dry separately. Stuff shoes with newspaper and air dry away from direct sunlight, which can cause yellowing and material breakdown.

## Rubber and Waterproof Soles

Clean outsoles with a stiff brush and soapy water to remove embedded debris from lugs and treads. Inspect periodically for wear patterns that may indicate the need for resoling. Waterproof membranes (such as Gore-Tex) should be reproofed periodically with a spray-on treatment once the fabric's water-beading effect diminishes, typically every 3 to 6 months with regular use.

## General Storage Tips

- Use shoe trees or stuff shoes with paper to maintain shape when not in use.
- Store in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight to prevent material fading and adhesive breakdown.
- Rotate between multiple pairs to allow cushioning materials to fully decompress and recover between wears.
- Avoid storing shoes in plastic bags for extended periods, as trapped moisture can encourage mold growth.
