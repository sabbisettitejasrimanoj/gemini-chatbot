# Footwear Sizing Guide

## How to Measure Your Foot

1. Stand on a piece of paper with your heel against a wall.
2. Mark the tip of your longest toe on the paper.
3. Measure the distance from the wall to the mark in centimeters.
4. Repeat for the other foot — many people have one foot slightly larger than the other. Use the larger measurement to choose your size.
5. Measure feet in the afternoon or evening, when they are typically at their largest due to natural swelling throughout the day.

## Width Guide

Footwear width is typically labeled as Narrow (B), Medium/Standard (D), or Wide (2E and up) for men's shoes, and Narrow (A), Medium (B), or Wide (D) for women's shoes. If the ball of your foot feels compressed in a standard width, consider sizing up in width rather than length, since a longer shoe with the wrong width can cause heel slippage.

## Fit Notes by Shoe Type

- **Running shoes**: Leave a thumb's-width of space (about 1 cm) between your longest toe and the end of the shoe to accommodate foot swelling during exercise.
- **Dress shoes**: Should fit snugly at the heel with minimal slippage, since leather uppers stretch slightly with wear.
- **Boots**: Account for thicker socks; consider sizing up half a size for winter boots worn with heavy socks.
- **Sandals**: The footbed should match the length of your foot without excessive overhang at the edges.

## When Between Sizes

If your foot length falls between two listed sizes, sizing up is generally recommended for athletic shoes to allow for swelling, while sizing down is generally recommended for dress shoes to minimize slippage — though this can vary by brand and last shape.
