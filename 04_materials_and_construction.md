# Footwear Materials and Construction Guide

## Upper Materials

**Full-grain leather** is the outermost layer of the hide, retaining the natural grain and strength of the material. It is durable and develops a patina over time but requires regular conditioning to stay supple.

**Suede** is leather that has been sanded on the flesh side to create a soft, napped texture. It is less durable and more water-sensitive than full-grain leather but offers a distinctive look.

**Engineered mesh** is a woven synthetic fabric used in athletic shoes for breathability and lightweight construction. Mesh density can be varied across zones of the shoe to balance ventilation with structural support.

**Knit uppers** are constructed using techniques similar to sock manufacturing, producing a seamless, sock-like fit. Knit uppers are popular in running and lifestyle shoes for their flexibility and lightweight feel.

**Canvas** is a plain-woven cotton fabric, valued for its breathability and casual aesthetic, commonly used in classic sneaker designs.

## Midsole Technologies

**EVA (ethylene-vinyl acetate) foam** is a lightweight, cushioned foam commonly used in midsoles. It offers good shock absorption but compresses and loses responsiveness faster than more advanced foams.

**PU (polyurethane) foam** is denser and more durable than EVA, offering better long-term cushioning retention, though it is heavier.

**TPU (thermoplastic polyurethane) foams**, marketed under various brand names, offer a high energy return and are often used in premium running shoes for their springy, responsive feel.

**Carbon fiber plates** are sometimes embedded within the midsole foam to increase propulsion and stiffness, primarily found in racing and performance running shoes.

## Outsole Materials

**Carbon rubber** is a hard-wearing rubber compound used in high-wear areas like the heel for durability.

**Blown rubber** is a softer, more cushioned rubber typically used in the forefoot for flexibility and ground feel.

**Gum rubber** is a natural rubber compound valued for its grip, especially in skate shoes and casual sneakers.

## Construction Methods

**Cement construction** bonds the upper to the outsole using adhesive; it is cost-effective and common in mass-market athletic and casual shoes.

**Goodyear welt construction** stitches the upper, insole, and a welt strip together, allowing the shoe to be resoled multiple times. This method is common in premium dress shoes and boots.

**Vulcanization** bonds the rubber outsole to the upper using heat and pressure, creating a flexible, durable bond common in canvas sneakers and skate shoes.
